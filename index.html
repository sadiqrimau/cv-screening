How it works

Three steps. Most roles come back the same day.

1

You set the rules

Minimum experience, required licences and certifications, visa or iqama status, languages, salary ceiling — whatever means "no" for this role.

2

Every CV gets read

Free text, PDF, inconsistent formatting, any layout. Knockouts apply first, then each surviving candidate is scored against your actual job description — not keyword-matched.

3

You get a defensible shortlist

Ranked candidates with a plain-English reason each, a full CSV of everyone screened with their reject reason, and borderline cases flagged for your review.

Why it holds up

  • Gulf-aware where it matters. SCFHS, DHA/DOH, QCHP, DataFlow verification, iqama transferability, Nitaqat and Emiratisation quota fit. No off-the-shelf tool encodes these.
  • It catches what a fast read misses. Expired certifications, licences listed as "in progress" being counted as held, and job-hopping patterns across dates.
  • Nothing is silently dropped. A strong candidate knocked out on one rule is flagged as possibly waivable, not deleted.
  • No protected characteristics. Nationality, age, gender and marital status are never extracted or scored — only experience, skills, certifications and availability. (Nationality is used solely where a client's quota requirement makes it a stated hiring criterion.)
  • A human reviews every shortlist before it reaches you. You are not receiving raw model output.
  • Your data doesn't linger. CVs are processed to produce your shortlist and deleted after delivery. Redacted CVs work fine — the scoring doesn't need names.
